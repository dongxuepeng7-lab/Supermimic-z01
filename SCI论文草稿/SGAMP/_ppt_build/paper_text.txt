Abstract—Endowing bipedal robots with natural yet robust
gaits is a central challenge. Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)
distill motion-capture style into a policy, but vanilla AMP
has three limitations: a physics-blind style reward, a phase-
agnostic discriminator that induces within-cycle jitter, and a
fixed task–style weight that conflicts on difficult terrain. We
proposeStability-Gated Adversarial Motion Priors(SG-AMP)
with three lightweight modules: (i) a Center-of-Pressure (CoP)
stability margin gates the style reward through a smooth sigmoid,
grounding style in physics; (ii) a foot-contact-driven gait phase
is injected into the discriminator for phase-consistent learning;
and (iii) the style weight follows an exponential moving average
of the stability margin, forming a self-paced curriculum. Using
only foot contact forces and foot-sole geometry from the physics
backend (Isaac Sim PhysX / MuJoCo), SG-AMP raises external-
push and rough-terrain survival to 0.736 and 0.486 (+59.3% and
+122.9% over AMP) and cuts spectral energy by 63.5%, with
ablations verifying each module.

I. INTRODUCTION
Reinforcement learning (RL) has produced robust
legged locomotion, from agile quadrupedal skills [1],
challenging-terrain traversal [2], and rapid adaptation [3],
to bipedal/humanoid locomotion from proprioceptive blind
walking [4], perceptive locomotion over irregular terrain [5],
[6], and general policies over challenging terrain [7], [8].
Recent humanoid controllers now transfer zero-shot to real
hardware [9] and enable whole-body imitation, teleoperation,
and unified multi-mode control [10], [11], [12], [13]. Yet the
resulting gaits are often stiff, bent-kneed, and lack biological
fluidity [14], because hand-crafted task rewards cannot
capture the subtle balance and coordination of human gait
[15].
Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) [15] distill motion-
capture (MoCap) style into a policy via a discriminator, and
remain the dominant route to naturalness [14], [16]. The AMP
lineage has since scaled into reusable skill embeddings (ASE)
[17], conditional latent models (CALM) [18], and universal
motion representations (PULSE) [19], yielding increasingly
fluid and controllable motion. However, vanilla AMP has three
well-documented limitations [14], [16]: (i) the discriminator
yields a physics-blind style reward carrying no stability in-
formation, so style can reward unstable motion; (ii) the prior
is phase-agnostic, so the style reward fluctuates within a gait
cycle and induces high-frequency jitter; and (iii) the fixed task–
style weight creates an objective conflict on difficult terrain,
where stability and style compete.
We proposeStability-Gated Adversarial Motion Priors(SG-
AMP), which inserts an automatic physics valve—the Center
of Pressure (CoP)—between the discriminator’s style signal
and the policy’s stability objective, while making the discrim-
inator phase-aware and the style injection self-paced. Our con-
tributions are three independently ablating modules: (1) aCoP-
gated style rewardthat gates the style reward by a sigmoid
of the CoP stability margin, grounding style in physics; (2)
aphase-conditioned discriminatorthat injects a foot-contact-
driven gait phase(cosϕ t,sinϕ t)for phase-consistent learning;
and (3) astability-driven adaptive style-weight curriculum
λt =λ max¯gt that resolves the style–stability conflict without
manual tuning. Unlike CoP used as a quadrupedtask reward
[20] or the task-side Foothold Penalty of T-GMP [16], we
are the first to use CoP stability as agate on the AMP style
reward. The method requires only foot contact forces and foot-
sole geometry, available from the physics backend (Isaac Sim
PhysX for training, MuJoCo for sim2sim), so we study it
purely in simulation on a self-developed biped.
II. RELATEDWORK
Following generative adversarial imitation learning [21]
and reference-motion imitation [22], AMP [15] brings GANs
to stylized control: a discriminator scores state transitions
and a style reward steers the policy to imitate reference
motion; for humanoids, retargeted MoCap is treated as real
and policy transitions as fake [14], [16]. Because the style
reward is an ambiguous scalar and adversarial training is
unstable, GMP [14] replaces the discriminator with a frozen
conditional V AE for dense joint/keypoint supervision, and T-
GMP [16] conditions this latent manifold on terrain and adds
a Foothold Penalty. In contrast, we keep the discriminator and
only modify its input (phase, contribution 2) and output usage
(CoP gating, contribution 1) at lower implementation cost.
For multi-terrain humanoid control, contrastive mixture-of-
experts methods [23], [8], model-assisted RL [24], and skill-
graph switching [25] organize skills and terrains orthogonally
to our focus on the style quality and stability of a single
gait. CoP-based stability is a classical criterion rooted in zero-
moment point (ZMP) theory [26], [27]: Hwang et al. [20] use
mind(p CoP,edges)as a quadrupedtask reward. We instead
use it as agate on the style reward, so naturalness and stability
cooperate rather than compete. Phase-based periodic rewards
have been used to learn common gaits [28]; we inject phase
into the AMPdiscriminatoras a structural prior rather than a
task reward.

C. Results
All policies are evaluated over 5 random seeds with zero-
shot transfer in MuJoCo. Under nominal flat walking every
method achieves100%survival, so flat survival is omitted
as non-discriminative. Table I reports push and rough-terrain
robustness plus spectral energy and planar touchdown.
a) Gait kinematics and contact stability:As shown in
the walking snapshots (Fig. 4), SG-AMP produces a fluid,
anthropomorphic gait with upright torso and natural swing
clearance, avoiding the excessive knee flexion of Pure RL.
In the MuJoCo contact analysis (Fig. 5), standard AMP
and Pure RL suffer ankle tilting at touchdown, yielding low
planar-touchdown ratios (46.8%/54.2%) and localized impact
forces that degrade push recovery (0.462) and rough-terrain
traversal (0.218). SG-AMP enforces planar foot alignment
(85.4%), lifting push survival by+59.3%and rough survival
by+122.9%over AMP.

Fig. 5. Foot-ground interaction and ground-reaction-force (GRF) analysis in

b) Ablation study:The lower partition of Table I vali-

push survival to0.512and planar touchdown to49.6%,
since unconstrained style rewards encourage unstable edge
landing—the core value of contribution 1. Removing phase
conditioning (−G2) inflates spectral energy from1.65to
3.08×10 −3 (+86.7%), confirming that phase conditioning
suppresses within-cycle discriminator noise and joint flutter.
Fixingλ t ≡λ max (−G3) degrades rough-terrain survival
to0.352, showing the curriculum avoids adversarial conflict
during early stabilization.
c) Gate behavior under stress:The adaptive gate acts
as intended: in steady flat walking the margin stays positive
(minimum+0.0086m) and the gate engages only8.4%of
stance (g <0.8); under severe pushes and irregular terrain
the minimum margin turns negative and the gate activates
for35.6%/44.2%of stance, suppressing style until stability
is regained.
V. CONCLUSION
We presented SG-AMP, which addresses three limitations
of vanilla AMP—a physics-blind style reward, a phase-
agnostic discriminator, and a fixed task–style weight—with
three lightweight, independently ablating modules: a CoP-
gated style reward, a phase-conditioned discriminator, and an
adaptive style-weight curriculum. Requiring only foot contact
forces and foot-sole geometry, SG-AMP suppresses joint flut-
ter, eliminates edge landing, and improves push and rough-
terrain robustness in simulation. Future work includes zero-
shot hardware transfer and perceptive multi-terrain extension.
